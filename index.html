Introduction

Our visualization uses the City of Chicago's Crimes - 2001 to Present dataset, which includes reported crime incidents throughout Chicago, Illinois. We specifically focused on home thefts in residences and apartments to compare how the incidents differ over time and across districts. The main variables we used were the year, location type, police district, and arrest status. Additionally, we calculated the total number of incidents and arrest rates to better reveal patterns in the data.

Design Decisions

We chose a line chart to display the changes in thefts and burglaries over time because it is easy to identify trends and compare residences with apartments. We also used a horizontal bar chart to compare incidents across the different Chicago police districts, allowing us to see which had higher or lower totals. For colors, we used orange and blue for both charts to make the categories easy to distinguish, even for those with many forms of color-blindness and it also matches the Chicago Bears colors. We considered using bar charts for the yearly trends but decided a line chart would be better to show the changes over time. For interactions, we implemented dropdown menus that allow users to filter by arrest status and switch between the number of incidents and arrest rate. Users can also hover over the charts to get more detailed information, click on a year to focus on it, and select a police district to filter the timeline. We chose these interactions because they allow users to explore specific parts of the dataset without making an overly complicated visualization.
