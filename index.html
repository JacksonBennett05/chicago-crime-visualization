A brief introduction to your dataset

our visualization uses the City of Chicago's Crimes - 2001 to Present, which contains reported crime incidents thoughout Chicago. We specifically focused on the home thefts occuring in residences and appartments to compare how the incidents differ over time and accross districts. The main varaibles we used were the year, location type, police district and arrest status. On top of that we also calcualted the total number of incidents and arrest rates to better understand the patterns in the data.

Rationale for your design decisions

We chose a line chart to display the changes in thefts and burglaries over time because it is easy to identify trends and compare residences with appartments. We also used a horizontal bar chart to compare incidents across the different Chicago police districts allowing to see which had higher or lower totals. For colors we used green and blue for both charts to make the categories easy to distinguish. We considered using bar charts for the yearly trends but decided a line chart would be better to show the changes over time. For interactions we implemented dropdown menus that allow users to filter by arrest status and switch between number of incidents and arrest rate. The users also can hover over the charts to get a more detailed information and click on a year to focus and select a police district to filter the timeline. We chose these interactions because they allow users to explore specific parts of the dataset without making an overly complicated visualization.
